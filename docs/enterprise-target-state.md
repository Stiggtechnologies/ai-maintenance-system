# SyncAI Enterprise Target State

## Positioning

SyncAI's primary category is:

> **Governed Industrial Engineering Intelligence for high-risk, asset-intensive operations.**

SyncAI is not generically a “mission assurance” product. Mission assurance is a possible defence or aerospace outcome. The common product value is better engineering decisions, safer technical change, stronger asset integrity, higher operational readiness, and verified reliability and production outcomes.

## Sector outcomes

| Sector | Primary outcomes |
| --- | --- |
| Energy and mining | Production assurance, asset integrity, process safety, reliability, maintenance effectiveness, and capital prioritization |
| Manufacturing | Equipment effectiveness, quality, throughput, maintenance readiness, and constraint management |
| Utilities and infrastructure | Service reliability, infrastructure resilience, emergency readiness, and lifecycle stewardship |
| Aerospace | Configuration assurance, system readiness, verification, reliability, and technical risk control |
| Defence | Equipment readiness, sustainment, configuration control, technical assurance, and mission assurance where contractually applicable |
| Transportation | Fleet readiness, safety, lifecycle cost, reliability, and service continuity |

## Target outcome

At completion, SyncAI should be the governed engineering and operational-intelligence layer that connects:

> approved requirements and engineering knowledge → asset and configuration state → operational evidence → deterministic analysis and AI reasoning → human technical authority → controlled work and change → verification → measured outcomes → organizational learning.

It should help users answer:

> **What is happening, why does it matter, what evidence supports it, what action is justified, who has authority, and did the approved action produce the intended outcome without increasing risk?**

## Non-negotiable principles

1. **Human technical authority remains accountable.** AI may detect, explain, calculate, simulate, recommend, draft, and route. It does not silently approve or perform high-consequence operational actions.
2. **Engineering authority is explicit.** Every source, threshold, model, requirement, calculation, and recommendation carries authority, applicability, revision, provenance, confidence, and review state.
3. **Deterministic engineering is separate from generative language.** Physics, rules, limits, constraints, and calculations are versioned, testable, and independently reviewable.
4. **Customer and program boundaries are enforced at every layer.** Tenancy, site, asset, program, classification, jurisdiction, contract, and need-to-know are derived from trusted identity and policy—not caller-supplied fields.
5. **Safety and security override optimization.** The platform must fail safely, abstain when evidence is insufficient, and preserve approved safeguards when dependencies are unavailable.
6. **The operational system remains authoritative.** SyncAI integrates through bounded, observable interfaces and does not become an uncontrolled control-system or command-system path.
7. **Learning never silently rewrites approved truth.** New evidence creates reviewable proposals, supersession decisions, and versioned changes.
8. **Verified outcomes matter more than generated recommendations.** Value and performance claims distinguish projected, observed, validated, and independently verified results.

## Target product capabilities

### 1. Enterprise engineering knowledge plane

The completed platform should provide:

- governed document and data ingestion;
- malware, content, classification, and disclosure scanning;
- source checksum and immutable provenance;
- canonical mapping to assets, components, failure modes, requirements, hazards, controls, calculations, procedures, and standards;
- authority, applicability, revision, supersession, confidentiality, and review states;
- hybrid lexical, semantic, graph, and deterministic retrieval;
- source-conflict detection and required engineering review;
- tenant- and asset-aware context packages;
- approved publication and retirement workflows;
- evidence-preserving export.

The knowledge plane must reuse the canonical asset, component, physics, requirement, evidence, work, and decision stores rather than duplicate them.

### 2. Digital Engineering DNA and twin factory

The completed twin capability should include:

- manufacturer-neutral asset-class templates;
- shared component DNA;
- OEM and model overlays;
- customer, site, duty, environment, and configuration overlays;
- approved thresholds and limits from controlled sources;
- installed configuration and modification state;
- functional, failure, inspection, maintenance, control, and protection models;
- telemetry and document mappings;
- versioned compilation with impact analysis;
- field validation and authorized approval;
- continuous reconciliation with approved operational and engineering systems;
- retirement and supersession history.

### 3. Trustworthy industrial data and metrology

SyncAI should determine whether evidence is fit for a particular decision before using it.

Required capabilities include:

- source-system authority and connector trust;
- sensor calibration status, due date, history, and traceability;
- units and controlled conversion;
- measurement uncertainty and confidence propagation;
- timestamp source, synchronization quality, sequence, and latency;
- bad-quality, substituted, stale, frozen, biased, drifting, and missing-value handling;
- historian compression and aggregation awareness;
- derived-tag lineage;
- reconciliation across conflicting sources;
- operating-state and configuration context;
- evidence suitability rules by decision risk class;
- explicit abstention or escalation when data is insufficient.

### 4. Engineering analysis and model assurance

The completed platform should combine:

- deterministic physics and engineering calculations;
- reliability, degradation, risk, and lifecycle models;
- simulation and scenario analysis;
- approved constraints and applicability envelopes;
- assumptions, uncertainty, units, and limitations;
- test vectors and regression suites;
- independent verification for critical models;
- model versioning, approval, deployment, monitoring, and retirement;
- shadow mode, canary evaluation, rollback, and post-change validation;
- out-of-distribution and contradictory-evidence handling;
- human escalation thresholds.

### 5. Governed AI workforce

The agent organization should operate through one canonical runtime with:

- trusted identity and tenant-derived scope;
- versioned task and output contracts;
- least-privilege tools;
- idempotency and bounded retries;
- human approval policies;
- traceable source context;
- runtime schema validation;
- model registry and approved model routing;
- cost, latency, quality, and risk monitoring;
- prompt-injection and retrieval-poisoning controls;
- separation of advisory, workflow, and execution authorities;
- complete run, tool, decision, and outcome records;
- independent evaluation and red-team evidence.

### 6. Technical change and configuration assurance

SyncAI should govern the complete technical-change lifecycle:

1. detect or propose a change;
2. establish the current approved baseline;
3. identify affected requirements, interfaces, hazards, controls, assets, documents, procedures, training, spares, and work;
4. classify risk and required authority;
5. manage technical queries, deviations, waivers, concessions, and temporary changes;
6. perform engineering and operational review;
7. record approvals and conditions;
8. implement through approved systems;
9. complete testing, pre-startup or return-to-service review;
10. update the baseline and affected records;
11. verify effectiveness and unintended consequences;
12. close or revert the change.

No AI recommendation should bypass customer Management of Change, functional-safety, configuration-control, or return-to-service authority.

### 7. Reliability, maintenance, and asset-integrity workflows

The completed platform should support:

- RAM and criticality onboarding;
- FMEA/FMECA, RCM, FRACAS, RCA, defect elimination, and bad-actor management;
- inspections and condition monitoring;
- maintenance-strategy development and optimization;
- work request, planning, scheduling, readiness, execution, closeout, and verification;
- spares and materials risk;
- barrier and critical-control assurance;
- lifecycle plans, obsolescence, and capital decisions;
- verified value and learning;
- customer-configured governance and approval chains.

### 8. Human-systems integration

The product should treat users, procedures, software, hardware, data, organization, and operating context as one system.

Target capabilities include:

- role, competency, qualification, and authorization checks;
- workload-aware prioritization;
- alarm and recommendation burden management;
- shift handover and operational briefing;
- fatigue-sensitive workflows where appropriate;
- explanation calibrated to the user's role and decision authority;
- comprehension and acknowledgement for critical information;
- automation-bias and over-reliance controls;
- accessible and mobile field workflows;
- intermittent/offline use with safe synchronization;
- training, simulation, and authorization renewal;
- usability testing in normal, abnormal, emergency, and degraded conditions.

### 9. Controlled experimentation and simulation

Before promotion to operational use, customers should be able to evaluate recommendations through:

- historical replay;
- shadow mode;
- what-if scenarios;
- counterfactual comparison;
- validated digital-twin simulation;
- synthetic failures and data-quality disturbances;
- operator-in-the-loop exercises;
- cyber and model red-team scenarios;
- failure injection and recovery exercises;
- comparison against current practice and approved baselines;
- measurable acceptance criteria;
- controlled promotion, rollback, and monitoring.

The intended progression is:

> Observe → explain → recommend → simulate → review → approve → implement → verify → learn.

### 10. Immutable technical-assurance evidence

For every significant recommendation, approval, technical change, or deployment, SyncAI should produce an evidence package containing:

- question, trigger, and decision scope;
- user, role, authority, and tenant context;
- asset and approved configuration state;
- source records, timestamps, quality, and lineage;
- applicable requirements, hazards, controls, standards, and procedures;
- retrieved knowledge, authority, revision, and conflicts;
- calculations, models, versions, assumptions, uncertainty, and limitations;
- AI prompts/context, provider/model version, evaluations, and structured output;
- reviewer comments, approvals, conditions, and residual risk;
- implementation and execution records;
- test and verification results;
- observed outcomes and value classification;
- final disposition, supersession, retention, and cryptographic integrity.

Evidence packs should be exportable for customer engineering review, regulators, auditors, incident investigators, insurers, and independent assessors.

### 11. Enterprise integration plane

The completed product should provide a small number of high-quality, certified connectors rather than a broad unvalidated catalog.

Required characteristics:

- read-only by default;
- versioned APIs and event contracts;
- trusted identity and credential isolation;
- customer-managed secrets and keys;
- source-system authority and canonical mappings;
- replay, idempotency, checkpointing, reconciliation, and error recovery;
- data-quality and latency observability;
- schema-change detection;
- integration test harnesses;
- customer acceptance evidence;
- export and exit support.

Priority connector families:

- historian and condition monitoring;
- CMMS/EAM;
- ERP and materials;
- document control and PLM;
- laboratory, GIS, safety, and planning systems;
- approved data brokers or OT gateways.

### 12. Secure deployment and operational resilience

Target deployment patterns may include hosted SaaS, dedicated cloud, customer-controlled cloud, private cloud, industrial edge, intermittently connected sites, and disconnected environments where justified.

Each supported pattern requires:

- a reference architecture and threat model;
- a documented trust boundary and shared-responsibility matrix;
- infrastructure as code;
- secure build and artifact provenance;
- SBOM and vulnerability management;
- customer-managed identity, keys, logs, and network controls where required;
- backup, restore, failover, recovery objectives, and tested continuity;
- observability, capacity, incident, and support procedures;
- patch and lifecycle commitments;
- data residency, sovereignty, retention, and deletion controls;
- independent testing and customer acceptance.

A code artifact called “edge” is not sufficient evidence of a supported edge deployment.

## Enterprise security target

The target security model includes:

- enterprise OIDC/SAML federation;
- phishing-resistant MFA and tenant policy enforcement;
- privileged-access management and just-in-time administration;
- role-, attribute-, policy-, site-, asset-, program-, classification-, and action-based authorization;
- machine and workload identities;
- customer-managed encryption keys where required;
- secrets management and key rotation;
- signed builds, provenance attestations, and SBOMs;
- SIEM, SOC, SOAR, case, and incident integration;
- tamper-evident audit and evidence records;
- data loss prevention and controlled model-provider access;
- penetration, adversarial, resilience, and recovery testing;
- vulnerability disclosure and response commitments.

## Assurance profiles

SyncAI should implement configurable profiles that map product controls and evidence to customer obligations without claiming blanket certification.

### Core industrial profile

- ISO 55000/55001-aligned asset-management evidence;
- quality, risk, change, configuration, and audit controls;
- OT-segmentation and read-only integration patterns;
- customer technical-authority and approval workflows.

### Energy and mining profile

- asset integrity and barrier assurance;
- Management of Change;
- process-safety and functional-safety interfaces where applicable;
- reliability, maintenance, environmental, and production evidence;
- IEC 62443-aligned OT-security mapping where required.

### Aerospace profile

- requirements and interface traceability;
- configuration management;
- verification and validation;
- model and software assurance;
- human-systems integration;
- technical-authority evidence.

### Defence profile

- contract-specific cybersecurity controls;
- controlled and specified information boundaries;
- data sovereignty and disclosure restrictions;
- controlled-goods and export-control processes where applicable;
- segregated program environments;
- independent assessment and authorization evidence;
- readiness and sustainment outcomes, including mission assurance only where relevant.

### AI assurance profile

- model purpose and risk classification;
- govern, map, measure, and manage evidence;
- evaluation thresholds and failure modes;
- human oversight and abstention;
- incident, rollback, retirement, and change procedures;
- critical-infrastructure-specific testing.

## Commercial and procurement target

Enterprise readiness requires more than technical features. The product package should include:

- clear scope and capability statements;
- reference architectures and data-flow diagrams;
- security, privacy, subprocessor, and data-use documentation;
- service levels, support, escalation, maintenance, and lifecycle policies;
- pilot and production statements of work;
- measurable acceptance criteria;
- customer and supplier responsibility matrices;
- vulnerability and patch commitments;
- independent assessment evidence;
- business continuity and exit assistance;
- clear data, model, derived-insight, and intellectual-property terms;
- transparent liability and high-consequence-use boundaries.

## Portfolio and executive value

At maturity, SyncAI should provide portfolio views that connect engineering evidence to:

- production and service risk;
- asset readiness and integrity;
- deferred risk and maintenance exposure;
- critical controls and safeguards;
- supply and skills constraints;
- capital priorities and lifecycle alternatives;
- outage and severe-event scenarios;
- verified reliability, availability, cost, safety, environmental, readiness, and production outcomes.

Every value claim should show whether it is projected, observed, customer-validated, or independently verified.

## Completion criteria

The enterprise target state should not be considered complete merely because features exist. Completion requires evidence that:

1. one canonical active schema and orchestration runtime exist;
2. every service derives tenant and authorization context from trusted identity;
3. the active migration chain and E2E suite reproduce from a clean environment;
4. no unsupported legacy function is deployed or reachable;
5. enterprise identity and authorization are independently tested;
6. governed engineering knowledge ingestion and asset-aware retrieval work end to end;
7. approved customer thresholds and configuration replace simulation defaults;
8. at least one historian and one CMMS/EAM connector are production accepted;
9. technical change and return-to-service workflows are operational;
10. measurement quality and evidence fitness are enforced;
11. model and deterministic calculations have approved validation evidence;
12. evidence packs reconstruct high-consequence decisions;
13. backup, recovery, resilience, incident, and support procedures are tested;
14. independent security testing is complete;
15. applicable certifications and customer authorizations are obtained;
16. customer outcomes are verified and referenceable;
17. current, pilot, target, prohibited, and certified claims remain clearly separated.
